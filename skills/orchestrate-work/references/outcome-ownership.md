# Outcome ownership

Use for broad, ambiguous work where reaching the goal requires ongoing judgment
about what to make, priorities, and how the pieces form a complete deliverable.
Duration or agent count alone does not establish this need. A difficult bug with a
defined desired behavior can use engineering decomposition directly.

## Give owners the whole goal

Assign one or more subagents to own the final deliverable's success throughout the
work. Choose roles for the responsibility: a product manager or CEO may
fit an app; an editor may fit a publication. Titles are optional. The mandate matters:
understand the user's goal, shape a coherent approach, guide priorities, evaluate
the integrated result, and close gaps before handoff.

Give owners the goal, known constraints, intended audience, expected delivery form,
and decision authority. Early acceptance criteria may need refinement as ambiguity
is resolved. Owners can develop and revise the approach within the user's scope;
consequential changes to that scope or commitments go through the coordinator.
Owning the outcome is continuing work, not merely producing an initial brief or a
final review report.

If several owners share the deliverable, distinguish their responsibilities and
decision authority and specify how disagreements reach the coordinator. Retain one
coordinator for shared decisions, dispatch, and integration. Owners request needed
work through that coordinator rather than starting competing orchestration trees.

## Keep ownership active during execution

Divide execution around both the goal's uncertainties and real parallelism. An
owner may need research, design, implementation, and domain expertise; choose only
what the task needs. Give specialists independently useful outcomes and clarify
dependencies. An owner does not replace engineering decomposition once an
implementation path is understood.

Have owners inspect integrated progress, revise priorities when evidence warrants
it, and identify gaps between completed assignments and the user's actual goal.
Carry the owner's decisions into subsequent assignments. Specialist completion or
passing technical checks alone does not establish a coherent, useful final result.
Use newly spawned reviewers for independent scrutiny of that result; its ongoing
owners are not independent reviewers.

## Own a deliverable that can be handed over

Within the requested scope, owners ensure the result is ready for publication or
release in its expected form. For an app, that may mean runnable source with the
instructions needed to build and use it, or a usable application with its essential
journeys checked. Match the evidence to the actual deliverable; do not impose every
possible release artifact or expand a requested prototype into production work.

Report what was delivered, how readiness was checked, and material gaps. Readiness
does not authorize publishing, deployment, or other external actions; carry those
out when the user's task authorizes them.
